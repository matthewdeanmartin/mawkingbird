Retrocomputing on Mastodon

An Amiga repair, a new Apple II program or a rescued workstation: find people keeping old computers useful and sharing how they work. Vintage hardware, historical software and the communities around them belong here.

Choose people and hashtags, then preview their public posts. Useful subject tags remain available even with no posts, so you can follow and help grow the conversations you care about.

Questions

Do I need an account?

You can browse and preview public posts without an account. Sign in with your Mastodon account to follow people and hashtags or create a list.

Does this include emulation and retro games?

Yes, when they connect to vintage computers, programming or preservation. Repairs, old operating systems, BBS communities and hardware recreations also belong here. General gaming nostalgia is outside this selection. Preview tags that contain ads or unrelated posts before following.

How do I get onto this list?

Share retrocomputing posts regularly and allow your server to suggest your profile. Recent activity, sustained topic fit and review determine the selection. For help or removal, contact @mawkingbird@mastodon.social.

How we choose

People are listed when they asked their server to make them discoverable, or when they are public figures with public evidence: a Wikipedia or Wikidata entry, a YouTube channel with 10,000 subscribers, or 2,500 followers on Mastodon.

An account that looks like it wants to stay out of view is left out: few followers, hidden follower lists, no profile picture, together with a request not to be indexed. Anyone who turns off discoverability is never listed.

A language model checks that each person really posts about the topic, quoting their own posts as evidence. Every account is checked again each month: quiet accounts drop off and come back when they post again.

Need help?

Questions, or listed here and would rather not be? Message us on Mastodon and you will be removed for good. @mawkingbird@mastodon.social
