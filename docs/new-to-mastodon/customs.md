# Mastodon customs worth knowing

Mastodon has a few habits that are not rules but that many people expect. Following them makes it more likely your posts are seen and welcomed.

## Describe your images

Many Mastodon users are blind or have low vision, and many others read descriptions to understand a picture. Add a description to every image you post. Some people do not boost images without one.

In Mawkingbird, every attachment has a description field beneath its preview. See [Write useful media descriptions](../posting/alt-text.md).

## Use content warnings for heavy or spoiler topics

A content warning hides a post behind a short summary until the reader chooses to open it. People use them for news about violence, politics, health, food, spoilers and other topics readers may want to choose to see.

Different servers have different expectations. Check your server's rules, and see [Add a content warning](../posting/content-warnings.md).

## Write hashtags in CamelCase

Write `#BirdWatching` rather than `#birdwatching`. Screen readers read the capitalized version as words instead of one long string.

## Introduce yourself

A first post tagged `#introduction` that says who you are and what you post about is a good way to be found. Follow back people who welcome you if their posts interest you.

## Boost what you like

There is no algorithm spreading posts for you. Boosts are how good posts travel, so boost generously. A favourite tells the author you liked it; a boost shows it to your followers.

## Choose who sees each post

Every post has a visibility setting. **public** posts can appear anywhere. **unlisted** posts (also called *quiet public*) can be read by anyone but stay out of some public discovery areas. **private** posts go to your followers and the people you mention. See [Choose who can see a post](../posting/visibility.md).
